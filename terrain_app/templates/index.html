YOUR LANDSCAPE, ANYWHERE

Export your terrain

Models use the current elevation multiplier and base. Choose a size for the longest horizontal edge.

Each button downloads one file. 3MF requires the Print palette, Solid base, and at most four distinct colors across surfaces and enabled features; assign its color parts to your filaments in the slicer. 3MF includes selected features as surface inlays and raised buildings. GLB includes selected map features and their colors. STL and OBJ contain terrain geometry. Heightmaps and arrays preserve original elevations. GLB uses meters. OBJ, GLB, and PNG embed metadata.

FOUR FILAMENTS · SIX SURFACES

Print palette

Choose a color for each mapped surface. Share colors between surfaces to use four filaments. Buildings color their footprints; snow uses mapped glaciers and permanent snow. Unmapped ground uses Dirt.

OSM land cover supplies discrete surface colors. Terrain detail controls the smallest colored region. Enabled map features use their chosen colors in 3MF, GLB, and preview images. Their colors count toward the four-filament limit. Use 3MF for separate color parts and assign those parts to your filaments in the slicer; GLB and preview images also retain these colors.
FRAME YOUR LANDSCAPE

Bounding coordinates

Coordinates describe the rectangle before rotation. Each side must be between 50 meters and 250 kilometers. Selections cannot cross the date line.

A world of possibilities.

  1. Frame a place. The selection frame stays fixed on screen. Pan the map beneath it, and use the +/− buttons or mouse wheel to zoom. Drag the right edge horizontally, bottom edge vertically to resize. Drag the lower-right corner to rotate the box around its center. The 1:1, 3:4, and 16:9 buttons snap its width-to-height ratio while keeping its center and rotation. Click a selected ratio again to swap portrait and landscape (3:4 ↔ 4:3 or 16:9 ↔ 9:16). Use the two-point button to click two locations: the box centers on their midpoint and rotates its horizontal axis through them, preserving its size. Presets, the address and city search, and US ZIP codes (five digits or ZIP+4) also work; a typed latitude, longitude pair in the search box jumps straight there.
  2. Generate the landscape. Python fetches real elevation data from the source you choose (Mapzen worldwide, or USGS 3DEP lidar-derived data for the United States) and samples a terrain grid. Smaller areas reveal more detail; a denser grid cannot add detail absent from the source.
  3. Make it yours. Change the palette, raise the elevation multiplier, choose mapped colors in the Print palette, or explore map, wireframe, contour, and elevation views. Check the map-feature layers you want and choose their colors: roads, train tracks, borders, water, city boundaries, park boundaries, and AT (Appalachian Trail) follow the terrain surface. Buildings use OSM footprints and mapped heights or levels; missing heights default to an illustrative six meters. These layers are included in 3MF, GLB models, and preview images.
  4. Follow a section. Move the profile slider to inspect any slice along the frame's axes. The chart always shows unexaggerated heights above sea level.
  5. Export. Download a closed STL or OBJ model, a colored GLB, or the original elevation grid. Disable Solid base for an open terrain surface.

The starter {{ presets[default_place].name }} model uses bundled real elevation data. New terrain, place search, ZIP lookup, map tiles, and feature layers need an internet connection. Features reflect OpenStreetMap coverage; administrative border definitions vary by country. Feature layers support areas up to 50 km per side. Terrain starts as a ground surface. Enable Buildings for approximate structures from OSM; trees are not modeled. Place search uses OpenStreetMap data through Nominatim. ZIP locations come from Zippopotam.us / GeoNames.

Reset frame restores its default size and zero rotation. The two-point button toggles alignment mode. Escape cancels it without changing the frame; selecting a preset or resetting the frame also exits this mode. You can also focus a resize handle and use arrow keys; hold Shift for larger steps. The corner rotates in 1° steps, or 15° with Shift; Home resets its angle. Hold Shift while dragging the corner to snap to 15°. Escape cancels an active resize or rotation.

The ground grid is for perspective. The north indicator accounts for selection rotation. Profiles follow the box’s edges; orbiting changes the model’s apparent direction. Base thickness is a percentage of the longest horizontal side.
